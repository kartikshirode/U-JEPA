# A JEPA-Based Continual-Learning Architecture with a Safe Fact-Intake Loop: Exhaustive Technical Research Report for a Zero-Budget Solo Researcher

## TL;DR
- **Build the "world-model core" as a frozen, off-the-shelf consistency engine, not a trained-from-scratch JEPA.** On a free Kaggle T4 (15 GB, 9 h), the only defensible "JEPA-based continual learning" prototype is one that (a) freezes a small open JEPA/encoder + a small frozen teacher LLM as the stable core, (b) keeps facts OUTSIDE the weights in a retrieval/codebook memory (GRACE/WISE-style), (c) uses the world model as a *plausibility/placement arbiter* and adds a *separate source-trust gate* for truth, and (d) implements merge→probe→rollback as adapter-add / memory-write + held-out regression probe + checkpoint-revert. Training V-JEPA or DreamerV3-default from scratch does NOT fit free compute; a *narrow* gridworld/Crafter world model does.
- **The single highest-novelty, genuinely-feasible contribution** is the *safe-intake loop itself*: a probe-gated, rollback-capable continual fact-injection system where a JEPA-style latent consistency model scores plausibility and a source-trust signal gates truth — this directly attacks the WISE "impossible triangle" and the model-editing "edit-induced degradation" failure mode that the literature documents but does not solve with an automatic abort/rollback mechanism.
- **Your existing orthogonal-LoRA (O-LoRA) zero-forgetting code is the right substrate for the "skills" half; the "facts" half should live in non-parametric memory.** The literature is unanimous that additive parametric edits (ROME/MEMIT) cannot retract contradicted beliefs and degrade under sequential edits, validating insights (d) and (e). RAG/latent-memory + a consistency probe is the route that fits both the science and the 15 GB budget.

## Key Findings

1. **JEPA predicts representations, not pixels or tokens.** I-JEPA (Assran et al., CVPR 2023, arXiv 2301.08243) and V-JEPA/V-JEPA 2 (arXiv 2506.09985) train a context encoder + predictor to predict the *embeddings* of masked regions produced by an EMA target encoder, with the target detached (stop-gradient). This is the formal mechanism behind your "world-model core that judges plausibility in latent space." It differs fundamentally from generative reconstruction (predict pixels/tokens) and autoregressive token prediction (predict next discrete symbol).
2. **V-JEPA 2 is open-weights and the ViT-L (300M) checkpoint runs on a T4; the ViT-g (1B) action-conditioned world model is the genuine "world model" but is heavier.** `facebook/vjepa2-vitl-fpc64-256` does feature extraction in a few GB of VRAM; attentive-probe / LoRA fine-tuning on frozen features fits 15 GB. V-JEPA 2-AC (built on ViT-g) does MPC planning via the cross-entropy method and runs planning on a single RTX 4090 (~16 s/action). License is the practical catch: the core V-JEPA 2 checkpoints are released for commercial+research use, but the `facebook/jepa-wms` physical-planning world-model checkpoints are CC-BY-NC-4.0 (non-commercial).
3. **Latent world models split cleanly into the "Dreamer" line (RSSM, latent imagination, RL) and the "JEPA" line (self-supervised feature prediction).** DreamerV3 (arXiv 2301.04104, Nature 2025) is the canonical consequence-predictor but its 200M default config OOMs a 24 GB 3090 — you must shrink it. A world model is precisely a *predictor of consequences/dynamics*; a knowledge base is a *store of facts*. Your architecture's separation of "stable consistency core" from "churning facts" is exactly this distinction and is well-motivated by the literature.
4. **The facts half is the hard, unsolved half — confirmed across editing, continual-learning, and memory literatures.** ROME/MEMIT do single/mass edits by treating MLPs as key-value stores, but (i) edits don't propagate to logically entailed facts ("ripple effect"; Cohen et al., TACL 2024, where MEND/ROME/MEMIT average <50% on ripple tasks and in-context editing beats them), (ii) sequential editing causes gradual then catastrophic forgetting (arXiv 2401.07453), and (iii) a single ROME edit can destabilize a model. WISE (NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2405.14768) proves an "impossible triangle" (reliability/generalization/locality cannot be realized together) for editing either parametric long-term memory or retrieval-based working memory, and resolves it with a *dual parametric memory* (main + side memory + router + knowledge-sharding) — a near-formal version of your base+delta intuition.
5. **GRACE (arXiv 2211.11031, NeurIPS 2023) already does rollback-friendly, weight-frozen, thousands-of-edits lifelong editing via a discrete key-value codebook in latent space.** Each edit is a codebook entry (key = cached layer activation, value = learned embedding) with a deferral radius ε; GRACE is "the first method enabling thousands of sequential edits" and deleting an entry is a clean rollback. This is the most directly reusable "merge + rollback" organ and validates that facts-in-working-state beats facts-in-weights for your loop.
6. **Self-editing/self-improving systems exist (SEAL, arXiv 2506.10943) but explicitly do NOT solve safe gating.** SEAL uses an RL loop (ReST-EM) to generate "self-edits" applied via SFT; on the no-passage SQuAD knowledge-incorporation variant it improves QA from a 33.5% baseline to 47.0% (single passage), beating GPT-4.1-generated synthetic data (46.3%), and reaches 72.5% on few-shot ARC vs 20% without RL and 0% without adaptation — but the authors flag catastrophic forgetting under continued self-editing. Combined with model-collapse theory (Shumailov et al., Nature 2024; "Strong Model Collapse," arXiv 2410.04840), this strongly justifies insight (f): chat/own-output learning must go through the same gate.
7. **Complementary Learning Systems (CLS) theory (McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly 1995; Kumaran et al. 2016) is the formal neuroscience backing for base+delta + consolidation/replay**, and has concrete deep-learning implementations (Deep Generative Dual Memory, arXiv 1710.10368; CLS-for-TD-learning, arXiv 1905.02636; HiCL, arXiv 2508.16651). This gives your "sleep/consolidation" step (insight e) a citable lineage.

## Details

### PART 1 — Deep background on JEPA

**The position paper.** Yann LeCun's "A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence" (OpenReview, 2022) frames JEPA as an energy-based model (EBM): a scalar energy is low for compatible (x, y) pairs and high for incompatible ones; the prediction error *is* the energy. The proposed full agent has a configurator, perception, a **world model** (the "core of intelligence" and primary JEPA domain — it infers missing information and predicts plausible future states given an action sequence, i.e., an internal simulator), a cost module (intrinsic cost + trainable critic), actor, and short-term memory. The hierarchical variant is H-JEPA. A pedagogical companion is Dawid & LeCun, "Introduction to Latent Variable Energy-Based Models" (arXiv 2306.02572, 2023). **This directly supports your framing: the world model is a consequence-predictor / consistency engine, separate from a fact store.**

**I-JEPA (image).** arXiv 2301.08243 (CVPR 2023). Mechanics: partition an image into patches; a ViT **context encoder** processes visible patches; a **predictor** (narrow ViT) conditioned on positional mask tokens predicts the representations of several large target blocks; the **target encoder** is an EMA copy of the context encoder, and targets are computed by masking *its output* (not the input). Loss = average L2 between predicted and target patch representations. Trained a ViT-Huge/14 on ImageNet with 16 A100s in under 72 h, ~5× fewer iterations than comparable methods. Anti-collapse comes from the EMA target + the asymmetric predictor (plus, in some variants, VICReg-style regularization). Official code: `facebookresearch/ijepa`.

**V-JEPA and V-JEPA 2 (video, world models).** V-JEPA 2 (arXiv 2506.09985, FAIR 2025): an encoder maps raw video to embeddings; a predictor takes a video embedding + context about what to predict and outputs predicted embeddings. Scaling: encoder from ViT-L (300M) to ViT-g (1B+); pretraining data VideoMix22M (22M clips, >1M hours); 252K iterations; progressive-resolution training (16 frames/256px → 64 frames/384px only in the cooldown) gave up to 8.4× GPU-time savings. Benchmarks: 77.3% top-1 on Something-Something-v2, and 39.7 recall@5 on Epic-Kitchens-100 action anticipation (ViT-g384), outperforming the 8B-parameter PlausiVL by 12.1 points (a 44% relative improvement). **V-JEPA 2-AC** is an action-conditioned world model post-trained on under 62 h of unlabeled Droid robot video on top of the frozen ViT-g; the predictor is a 24-layer, 16-head, 1024-hidden block-causal transformer; it does zero-shot pick-and-place via MPC + the cross-entropy method, planning on a single RTX 4090 at ~16 s/action. HF: `facebook/vjepa2-vitl-fpc64-256` and the V-JEPA 2 collection. The `facebook/jepa-wms` repo ("What Drives Success in Physical Planning…") hosts JEPA-WM checkpoints (CC-BY-NC-4.0). V-JEPA 2.1 (arXiv 2603.14482) adds a dense predictive loss and deep self-supervision, scales to ViT-G 2B, hits 40.8 Recall@5 on Epic-Kitchens and a 20-point real-robot grasping gain over V-JEPA 2-AC, and releases distilled ViT-B/L (80M/300M) variants useful for tight VRAM.

**Other JEPA variants.** A-JEPA (audio), S-JEPA (signals/speech), MC-JEPA (motion+content), T-JEPA, and recent **VL-JEPA** (vision-language, arXiv 2512.10942) predict target text embeddings instead of reconstructing tokens. For language: **LLM-JEPA** (Huang, LeCun, Balestriero; arXiv 2509.14252) adds a JEPA loss to next-token loss using paired "views" (e.g., a text issue and its code diff); it outperforms standard fine-tuning across NL-RX, GSM8K, Spider, RottenTomatoes on Llama-3, OpenELM, Gemma2, Olmo families and is robust to overfitting. Code: `rbalestr-lab/llm-jepa`. This is the most directly relevant text-JEPA prior art for you, and a strong citation anchor for the "JEPA-based" framing of a language-domain architecture.

**LeJEPA and SIGReg.** LeJEPA (Balestriero & LeCun, arXiv 2511.08544, 2025) reframes anti-collapse as *distribution matching*: embeddings should follow an isotropic Gaussian to minimize downstream prediction risk, enforced by **Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg)**, which tests random 1-D projections (Epps–Pulley normality statistic, Cramér–Wold principle), achieving linear memory/compute and removing the need for stop-gradient, EMA teacher, or special normalization — a single hyperparameter λ. It trained ~50 models across 8 architecture families stably. Follow-ups: Weak-SIGReg (arXiv 2603.05924), "When Does LeJEPA Learn a World Model?", LeWorldModel/LeWM, Rectified LpJEPA. The reference implementation is only a few dozen lines — **a strong, cheap anti-collapse recipe if you train any small JEPA yourself.**

**Related self-supervised methods (anti-collapse).** Contrastive (SimCLR, negatives); BYOL (online/target EMA, no negatives); SimSiam (stop-gradient alone is crucial); Barlow Twins (cross-correlation → identity); VICReg/VICRegL (invariance + variance + covariance terms); DINO/DINOv2 (self-distillation with centering+sharpening). JEPA's distinctive choice is to predict in a *learned latent space* with a detached target rather than in input space — which is why it produces semantic representations cheaply and is the natural substrate for a "plausibility in latent space" engine.

### PART 2 — Deep background on world models

Classic: **Ha & Schmidhuber (2018)** "World Models" (VAE vision + MDN-RNN memory + small controller, trained in a "dream"). **PlaNet** (Hafner 2019, latent planning) → **Dreamer/V2/V3** (arXiv 1912.01603, 2010.02193, 2301.04104): a Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) encodes observations into latent states, a transition model predicts future latents, and an actor-critic learns purely in imagined latent rollouts. DreamerV3 (Nature 2025) is one general config across 150+ tasks and the first to mine Minecraft diamonds from scratch; **Dreamer 4** (arXiv 2509.24527) adds a shortcut-forcing objective and trains agents inside scalable world models entirely offline. Transformer world models: **IRIS** (discrete tokens + autoregressive transformer). Generative interactive environments: **Genie / Genie 2** (foundation world models that generate playable environments from prompts/images; controllable video models like Genie 3 struggle with precise physics and need many GPUs per scene). JEPA-line world models: V-JEPA 2-AC, DINO-WM, PLDM, and the LeWM/LeJEPA-world-model thread. **Definitional clarity for your paper:** a *world model* predicts consequences/dynamics (state, action → next state/representation) and is a *consistency/dynamics engine*; a *knowledge base* stores discrete, arbitrary facts and is a *truth store*. They have different update semantics — this is the conceptual spine of your design, supported by both LeCun's framing and the editing-vs-RAG literature.

### PART 3 — The facts half: continual learning, knowledge editing, RAG, safe updating

**Continual / lifelong learning & catastrophic forgetting.** Regularization: **EWC** (Kirkpatrick et al., PNAS 2017) penalizes changes to weights important for old tasks (penalty = Σ F_i(θ_i − θ*_i)², F = diagonal Fisher); effective but struggles when representations must change a lot, and has a known gradient-vanishing weakness (EWC-DR, arXiv 2603.18596). **Synaptic Intelligence**, **MAS**, **LwF** (knowledge distillation), experience/generative **replay**, orthogonal-gradient methods, and parameter-isolation/expansion (Progressive Networks). **LoRA-based continual learning is your substrate:** **O-LoRA** (arXiv 2310.14152, EMNLP-Findings 2023) learns each task in a low-rank subspace kept *orthogonal* to previous tasks' subspaces, requires no replay and no task-ID at inference — exactly your "zero forgetting on 2 tasks" result. Extensions: **N-LoRA** (nullspace), **OLoRA** (QR-orthonormal init), **OPLoRA** (orthogonal projection, arXiv 2510.13003), **OLieRA** (Lie-group multiplicative updates, arXiv 2509.06100). **Lesson:** orthogonal-LoRA elegantly handles the *skills* half (insight a); it does not solve the *facts* half because facts are arbitrary and mutually contradictory, not smooth task subspaces.

**CLS theory (the formal base+delta).** McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly (1995, *Psych. Review*); Kumaran et al. (2016): a fast hippocampal system (sparse, pattern-separated, episodic) + a slow neocortical system (distributed, generalizing), with hippocampal **replay during sleep** interleaving old and new memories to consolidate without forgetting. Computational instantiations: Deep Generative Dual Memory (arXiv 1710.10368, generative replay during simulated sleep), CLS-for-TD (arXiv 1905.02636), FearNet, HiCL (arXiv 2508.16651, two-phase specialize→consolidate schedule). **This is the citable theory behind your stable-core + churning-delta + consolidation design.**

**Knowledge editing / model editing.** **ROME** (locate-then-edit one MLP layer as KV store), **MEMIT** (mass-edit across several MLP layers — "spreads the signal across the network"), **MEND** (hypernetwork generates rank-decomposed weight updates), **KN** (knowledge neurons), **SERAC** (semi-parametric: external memory + scope classifier + counterfactual model), **GRACE** (discrete KV codebook adapter in latent space, weights frozen, thousands of sequential edits), **WISE** (dual parametric memory + router + knowledge-sharding). Scale/failure modes: ROME/MEMIT do well on single-edit reliability but score below 50% on ripple-effect/multi-hop tasks (RIPPLEEDITS/MQuAKE), and in-context editing (ICE) often beats parametric editing on ripple benchmarks (Cohen et al., TACL 2024); sequential editing → gradual + catastrophic forgetting (arXiv 2401.07453); a single ROME edit can collapse a model. Benchmarks: **zsRE**, **CounterFact**, **MQuAKE**, **KnowEdit** (aggregator), via the **EasyEdit** library (`zjunlp/EasyEdit`). **Compute reality (EasyEdit's own table):** LLaMA-2-7B editing needs 40–60 GB (FT 60 GB, SERAC 42 GB, IKE 52 GB, MEND 46 GB); only **GPT-2-XL (1.5B)** class fits a T4 (FT ~7 GB, SERAC/IKE ~10 GB). **This is decisive for your model-size choice — any parametric-editing baseline you run must be ≤1.5B.**

**RAG as "facts live in working state, not weights."** Standard RAG; **latent/embedding-space RAG** (inject retrieved KV states or latent vectors rather than text): TurboRAG (precomputed KV caches, arXiv 2410.07590), CacheGen, KVLink (arXiv 2502.16002), Cache-Augmented Generation, and **LAnR** (latent abstraction for RAG — a [PRED] token's hidden state produces dense retrieval vectors and an entropy-aware MLP decides when to stop retrieving, arXiv 2604.17866). **This is the cleanest place to put facts on free compute: a vector store + latent injection, where deletion = clean retraction (solving insight d).**

**Belief revision / unlearning.** Additive layers/adapters can *introduce* a fact but cannot *retract* a distributed contradicted belief (your insight d) — this is why machine unlearning is hard: knowledge is smeared across parameters, so deletion either under-removes or damages neighbors. Practical override beats deletion: route queries to a fresh memory entry that supersedes the old belief (GRACE codebook entry, WISE side memory, or a retrieval record marked "supersedes X"). Non-monotonic reasoning supplies the "new fact retracts old default" semantics you want.

**Source-trust / fact verification.** **FEVER** (Thorne et al., NAACL 2018, arXiv 1803.05355): 185,445 claims generated by altering Wikipedia sentences, labeled SUPPORTED / REFUTED / NOTENOUGHINFO (annotators reached 0.6841 Fleiss κ); the canonical pipeline is document retrieval → sentence selection → NLI/entailment, and the best full-pipeline labeling accuracy with correct evidence was only 31.87% (50.91% ignoring evidence), so it is a genuinely hard truth-gating testbed. Variants: CLIMATE-FEVER, COVID-Fact, FEVEROUS. **Use FEVER + a source-credibility/provenance score as the truth gate, distinct from the plausibility score.** This operationalizes insights (b)/(c): the world model checks *consistency/plausibility*, FEVER-style verification + source trust check *truth*; the novelty-vs-plausibility tension means the world model must never be the *sole* gate (genuine new facts look implausible; good hoaxes look plausible).

**Model collapse.** Shumailov et al., "AI models collapse when trained on recursively generated data," *Nature* 631(8022):755–759 (2024), show that "indiscriminate use of model-generated content in training causes irreversible defects in the resulting models, in which tails of the original content distribution disappear"; "Strong Model Collapse" (arXiv 2410.04840) shows even a non-vanishing fraction of synthetic data degrades models. **This justifies routing chat/own-output learning through the same verified-source + probe gate (insight f).**

**Probing / error signals.** Build a frozen held-out probe suite: (i) a *consistency probe* (NLI/entailment or the JEPA energy on a battery of (premise, consequence) pairs the core should rate plausible/implausible); (ii) a *perplexity/regression probe* (held-out general text — perplexity must not rise beyond a threshold τ); and (iii) a *behavioral test suite* (a fixed set of QA pairs covering previously-learned facts/skills — the "locality"/"retention" set). A merge that raises perplexity > τ, flips > k locality answers, or worsens consistency-AUROC triggers rollback. This is the "probe that detects degradation" organ and is your clearest novel contribution: editing papers *measure* degradation post-hoc but do not *gate and auto-revert* on it online.

### PART 4 — All the ways to build this

**World-model core options.**
- **(a) Cheap routes (RECOMMENDED).** Freeze a small open JEPA encoder (V-JEPA 2 ViT-L for vision; LLM-JEPA-style or a frozen small LLM's hidden states for text) and use it only to produce latents + a plausibility/energy score. Even cheaper and very defensible: use an **NLI/entailment model** (e.g., a DeBERTa-MNLI) or a small energy-based scorer as the "consistency engine" — it literally outputs entailment/contradiction, which is your placement/plausibility signal, and runs in under 2 GB. Distilling a larger teacher into a tiny consistency head is feasible in one session.
- **(b) Train a small/narrow genuine world model from scratch on a T4.** Feasible domains in 9 h/15 GB: **MiniGrid/BabyAI** (symbolic, CPU-cheap rollouts), **Crafter** (64×64 RGB, 1M-step budget — explicitly designed to avoid Atari's "2000+ GPU-days"), or procedurally generated text "micro-dynamics." Use a shrunk DreamerV3 config (size12m/size50m, reduced batch_size and train_ratio) with checkpoint-resume across sessions — the 200M default OOMs even a 24 GB 3090, so default is off-limits. A narrow JEPA trained with LeJEPA/SIGReg on one domain is the most "honest JEPA from scratch" option. **Honesty bar:** a world model trained on MiniGrid/Crafter is a *narrow-domain* world model; claim plausibility judgments only within that domain.
- **(c) V-JEPA 2 open-weights fallback.** ViT-L inference/probe fits 15 GB comfortably; ViT-g is tight but possible for inference. Vision-only; watch the CC-BY-NC caveat on JEPA-WM checkpoints.

**Where facts should live.** In-weights editing (ROME/MEMIT) fails insights (b)(c)(d) — it can't retract and degrades under sequential edits. Best fit: **in-working-state** (latent RAG / GRACE codebook / WISE side memory) with the core acting as placement arbiter. A hybrid (consolidate stable, high-trust facts into an orthogonal-LoRA "skills-ish" adapter during a periodic "sleep," keep volatile facts in memory) directly addresses insight (e), the accumulation/consolidation problem.

**Merge mechanisms:** (i) write a memory entry (cheapest, cleanest rollback); (ii) GRACE codebook insert; (iii) add a tiny per-fact LoRA/adapter; (iv) MEMIT-style weight edit (worst for rollback). **Probe/error signal:** perplexity-on-held-out + locality-flip-count + consistency-AUROC, thresholded. **Rollback:** memory-entry delete (O(1)), adapter remove, or checkpoint-and-revert (snapshot before merge). **The world model arbitrates placement/consistency (does this fact cohere?), never truth (is it real?) — truth comes from the source-trust + FEVER gate.** This is the precise mechanism that avoids insight (g)'s failure of "relocating the hard part": truth is explicitly assigned to a source-trust component, not smuggled into the world model.

### PART 5 — The best 3 end-to-end pipelines (ranked by feasibility × novelty)

**Pipeline 1 — "Probe-Gated Latent-Memory Continual Learner" (RECOMMENDED; highest feasibility, strong novelty).**
- **Architecture.** Frozen small LLM core (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, Apache-2.0, or Llama-3.2-1B) as decoder/teacher. Facts live in a **vector memory** with latent injection (retrieved KV / soft-prompt vectors) à la GRACE/latent-RAG. A frozen **NLI/entailment consistency model** + a JEPA-style latent scorer judges plausibility/placement. A **source-trust gate** (whitelist + FEVER-style verification model) decides truth-admissibility. **Merge** = memory write (+ optional per-fact LoRA). **Probe** = held-out perplexity + locality QA + consistency-AUROC. **Rollback** = delete memory entry / revert snapshot.
- **Trained vs frozen.** Core LLM and NLI model frozen; train only the small consistency/plausibility head, the router, and (optionally) per-fact LoRA adapters.
- **Datasets/benchmarks (free).** zsRE + CounterFact (edit success, locality), KnowEdit (lifelong), MQuAKE (ripple/multi-hop), FEVER (truth gate), WikiText (perplexity probe).
- **Metrics.** Edit success (reliability), locality/specificity, generalization, **forgetting** (backward transfer / retention on held-out QA), ripple accuracy, **probe AUROC for degradation detection**, **rollback effectiveness** (fraction of bad merges caught + reverted), false-abort rate.
- **Compute.** GPT-2-XL or 1.5B-class fits T4 comfortably (editing baselines ≤1.5B per EasyEdit); the full loop over a few hundred edits runs in under 9 h; multiple seeds across the 30 h/week budget.
- **Novelty claim.** "A JEPA-style latent-consistency-gated, rollback-capable continual fact-intake loop that separates plausibility (world model) from truth (source trust) and *automatically aborts and reverts* degrading merges — turning the WISE impossible triangle and edit-induced degradation into a monitored, recoverable process."
- **Likely failure mode.** Probe insensitivity (misses subtle degradation) or over-triggering (rejects good edits). **Fallback.** Pure GRACE codebook + perplexity probe; report the loop as an evaluation harness even if gating is imperfect.

**Pipeline 2 — "Narrow JEPA World Model as Plausibility Arbiter" (medium feasibility, higher conceptual novelty as a *genuine* JEPA).**
- Train a small JEPA from scratch with LeJEPA/SIGReg on **Crafter or MiniGrid** dynamics (predict next-state latent from state+action). Use its predictor energy as the plausibility score for candidate "facts" expressed as state transitions ("doing X causes Y"). Facts/edits live in a transition memory; merge/probe/rollback as in Pipeline 1 but in the gridworld domain. Decoder optional (render predicted latent to a sketch, as I-JEPA does).
- **Compute.** Shrunk model, checkpoint-resume across 9 h sessions; SIGReg is cheap (linear).
- **Novelty.** "A from-scratch JEPA world model used as an explicit consistency gate for continual knowledge intake in a controllable environment, with measured novelty-vs-plausibility trade-off curves."
- **Failure mode.** Domain too narrow to interest reviewers; latent collapse (mitigated by SIGReg). **Fallback.** Use V-JEPA 2 ViT-L frozen features instead of training from scratch.

**Pipeline 3 — "Multimodal Vision-Gated Intake" (lowest feasibility for v1; highest scope).**
- V-JEPA 2 ViT-L (vision) + frozen small LLM (text), each projected to a common latent the core reads; facts about images verified against the vision encoder's plausibility. Vision-input only, no image generation.
- **Compute.** Tight but possible for inference/probe; fine-tuning limited to small alignment heads.
- **Novelty.** Cross-modal plausibility gating. **Failure mode.** Alignment-head training instability on T4; modality gap. **Fallback.** Collapse to Pipeline 1 (text-only) for the first paper; keep vision as "future work."

**Ranking:** 1 > 2 > 3 for a zero-budget solo workshop paper. Pipeline 1 maximizes defensible novelty per unit compute risk; Pipeline 2 is the better "true JEPA" story if a reviewer demands a trained world model; Pipeline 3 is a stretch goal.

### PART 6 — Landscape of existing/in-progress projects to learn from

- **WISE (NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2405.14768).** Tried: dual parametric memory (main + side) + router + knowledge-sharding for lifelong editing. Worked: broke the impossible triangle better than ROME/MEMIT/GRACE on several settings. Limitation: side-memory capacity is finite; routing errors. **Lesson:** adopt side-memory + router; add the probe/rollback they lack.
- **GRACE (NeurIPS 2023, arXiv 2211.11031).** Tried: discrete KV codebook, frozen weights, thousands of edits, ε-ball generalization. Worked: best retention vs forgetting on zsRE/SCOTUS. Limitation: ε-ball generalization is brittle; codebook grows unbounded. **Lesson:** reuse as your merge+rollback organ; tune ε; cap/compact the codebook.
- **SEAL (MIT, 2025, arXiv 2506.10943).** Tried: RL (ReST-EM) self-edits applied via SFT, with a Qwen2.5-7B + LoRA inner loop. Worked: 33.5→47.0% single-passage knowledge incorporation (43.8% in the n=200 continued-pretraining setting, beating GPT-4.1 synthetic data), 72.5% few-shot ARC. Limitation: catastrophic forgetting under continued self-editing; needs 2×A100. **Lesson:** SEAL is the *ungated* version of your loop — your contribution is the safety gate + rollback, made cheap; cite it as motivation and as the thing you make safe.
- **Model-editing-at-scale (arXiv 2401.07453).** Showed gradual→catastrophic forgetting from sequential ROME/MEMIT on GPT-2-XL and GPT-J. **Lesson:** never rely on accumulating parametric edits (insight e); this is your strongest "why" citation.
- **Coconut (arXiv 2412.06769)**, plus **LatentMAS / latent multi-agent**, **Self-RAG**, **LLM-JEPA (2509.14252)**: continuous-latent reasoning and latent-space operation prior art. **Caveat from "Do Latent Tokens Think?" (arXiv 2512.21711):** Coconut latent tokens showed under-5% perturbation-success-rate vs up to 50% for CoT in LLaMA-3-8B — latent reasoning may be less causally load-bearing than hoped. **Lesson:** keep your latent operations *probed and measured*; don't assume latent = meaningful.
- **DINO-WM, PLDM, LeWM/LeJEPA-world-model thread, "When Does LeJEPA Learn a World Model?"** Active 2025–2026 work on stable latent world models; SIGReg is the current best-practice anti-collapse recipe. **Lesson:** cite and reuse SIGReg.
- **DreamerV3 variants (Curious-Replay).** **Lesson:** single-GPU world-model training is feasible only at small config + checkpointing; budget around the 9 h session cap.

**Differentiation:** none of these combine (world-model-as-plausibility-arbiter) + (separate source-trust truth gate) + (held-out probe that *auto-detects degradation*) + (one-fact rollback) into a single continual loop. That combination is your defensible delta — design the paper to highlight it explicitly rather than duplicating WISE/GRACE/SEAL.

### PART 7 — The three open design questions, answered in context

**Q1 — Input encoder pathway. Recommendation: (a) reuse frozen teacher LLM input-side blocks for text + frozen V-JEPA 2 for vision, with small trained projection heads to a common space.** Justification: on 15 GB you cannot train a unified multimodal encoder from scratch (option b) to any quality, and per-modality JEPA-from-scratch (option c) burns your whole compute budget on the encoder rather than the novel loop. Freezing pretrained encoders and training only lightweight projection/alignment heads is the standard, cheap, robust choice and keeps the "stable core" genuinely stable (the entire point of your architecture). For v1, go text-only: the teacher LLM's own embeddings *are* the latent the core reads — no projection needed.

**Q2 — Decoder structure. Recommendation: (a) the frozen teacher model's own LM head + final norm for v1; escalate to (c) a small purpose-built decoder only if the core's latents drift off-manifold.** Justification: if the core *is* the frozen teacher (Pipeline 1), its latents are already on-manifold and the native LM head decodes them for free. The off-manifold risk you flagged is real only when the core is a *separate* JEPA whose latent space ≠ the decoder's; in that case train a small adapter/decoder (option c) once, on frozen-core outputs, to map core latents → teacher input space (this is also how you'd bolt a JEPA world model onto a text decoder). Avoid (b) top-K blocks: more compute, little gain over (a)/(c).

**Q3 — Vision/image. Recommendation: (c) vision-INPUT only via a frozen V-JEPA 2 sub-encoder, NO image generation in v1.** Justification: image generation (options a/b) adds a decoder you must train and evaluate, multiplying compute and failure surface, and is irrelevant to demonstrating the *safe fact-intake loop*, which is your actual contribution. A first publishable prototype should be text-only or text+vision-input; defer all generation to future work. This keeps you inside the 9 h/15 GB envelope and focuses reviewer attention on the novel loop.

## Recommendations

1. **Build Pipeline 1 first.** Frozen Qwen2.5-1.5B (Apache-2.0) core, vector memory with latent injection, frozen NLI consistency model, FEVER + whitelist truth gate, perplexity + locality + consistency probe, memory-delete / snapshot rollback. Reuse your O-LoRA code for the periodic "consolidation/sleep" of high-trust facts into a skills adapter. **Benchmark on zsRE / CounterFact / KnowEdit / MQuAKE + WikiText perplexity + FEVER.**
2. **Stage the work.** (i) Reproduce GRACE/WISE-style editing on GPT-2-XL on the T4 (sanity + baseline). (ii) Add the probe and show it detects edit-induced degradation (arXiv 2401.07453 is your target phenomenon). (iii) Add auto-rollback and report % bad merges caught/reverted vs false-abort rate — *this table is your paper's core result*. (iv) Add the source-trust gate and show the novelty-vs-plausibility trade-off (genuine-but-implausible vs plausible-hoax injections).
3. **Thresholds that change the plan.** If probe AUROC for degradation detection < ~0.7, the gate is the bottleneck → invest in a better probe (more behavioral tests, a calibrated consistency scorer) before adding modalities. If single-T4 wall-clock for the full loop > 9 h, cut edit count or model size (GPT-2-XL) and use checkpoint-resume across sessions. If you want a "true JEPA" reviewer story, run **Pipeline 2** (Crafter/MiniGrid + LeJEPA/SIGReg) as a second experiment.
4. **Licensing/compute hygiene.** Prefer Apache-2.0 weights (Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B/7B, Pythia) and MIT (GPT-2) to keep the artifact publishable; avoid CC-BY-NC JEPA-WM checkpoints if you want a permissive release. Use NF4/QLoRA (7B is the practical QLoRA max on 15 GB; 3B is the safe max on the 8 GB laptop), gradient checkpointing, fp16 (not bf16 on the T4's Turing architecture), and batch_size 1 with grad-accumulation where needed.
5. **Write the paper around the loop, not the core.** The novelty is the *safe-intake loop with automatic degradation-detection and rollback* and the *plausibility-vs-truth separation* — not a new encoder. Frame it as "JEPA-based continual learning" via the latent-consistency arbiter; cite LLM-JEPA, WISE, GRACE, SEAL, and model-editing-at-scale as the immediate context.

## Caveats
- **Compute numbers for V-JEPA 2 inference VRAM are estimates** from parameter counts + the confirmed 8192×1024 activation shape; no official per-model VRAM table exists. The "V-JEPA 2-AC plans on a single RTX 4090 at ~16 s/action" figure IS from the paper. DreamerV3's "200M config OOMs a 24 GB 3090" is a maintainer GitHub report and is the decision-relevant hard data point — the default config will not fit a T4.
- **"World model" claims must be scoped honestly.** A frozen NLI model or a Crafter-trained JEPA is a *narrow* consistency engine, not a general world model; say so explicitly. The novelty-vs-plausibility tension (insight c) means your plausibility gate will have irreducible error on genuinely novel true facts and on well-crafted hoaxes — measure and report this, don't hide it.
- **Latent reasoning may be less meaningful than it looks** (arXiv 2512.21711 on Coconut) — keep every latent operation probed and ablated rather than assumed.
- **Knowledge editing on 7B+ is infeasible on free compute** (EasyEdit: 40–60 GB); you are restricted to ≤1.5B-class for any parametric-editing baseline. This is a hard constraint, not a footnote.
- **SEAL and model-collapse results jointly imply** that any self-/chat-learning you add must pass the same verified-source + probe gate; do not let the model train on its own unverified outputs.
- This report prioritizes arXiv/peer-reviewed sources; a few feasibility figures rely on practitioner blogs (RunPod, Unsloth-based guides, Modal) that are mutually consistent but not peer-reviewed. Two cited preprints carry future-dated arXiv IDs (e.g., V-JEPA 2.1 2603.x, EWC-DR 2603.x, "Do Latent Tokens Think?" 2512.x) reflecting very recent/in-press work; treat their specific numbers as provisional until the camera-ready versions are checked.
